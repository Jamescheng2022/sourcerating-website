---

An overseas hardware startup, mid-tier equipment builder, or industrial component brand designs a custom engineered part—a precision-machined valve body, an IP67 electronics enclosure, a multi-cavity injection-molded housing, or an automated robotic subassembly. Before emailing 3D CAD STEP files and full engineering drawings to prospective factories in Shenzhen, Ningbo, Dongguan, or Changzhou for quotation, the procurement director insists on prudence: the factory must sign the company's standard Two-Page Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA), drafted by corporate counsel in California, Delaware, or London.

The sales manager in China signs, stamps the document with a red company chop, and returns the scan within four hours. The buyer feels protected. Three months later, the mold is fabricated, first-article samples are approved, and purchase orders are placed.

Eighteen months later, disaster strikes across three fronts:
1. The buyer discovers their exact product listed on Alibaba, AliExpress, and Amazon at a 45% discount, sold by an unfamiliar trading firm whose registered address matches the factory’s warehouse.
2. When the buyer attempts to switch suppliers due to declining quality, the factory manager refuses to release the injection molds, claiming: *"The tooling belongs to our factory because you only paid for tooling development, and you owe us storage fees under our statutory retaining lien."*
3. When the buyer threatens litigation under the Delaware-law NDA, their legal counsel delivers the coup de grâce: judgments issued by U.S. or UK courts cannot be directly enforced in Mainland China without a bilateral recognition treaty, the NDA only prohibited *disclosing* information to third parties (not *using* it internally or *selling* to the buyer's customers), and proving unliquidated "actual economic damages" in an overseas court is commercially impossible.

This is the classic Western NDA trap in cross-border manufacturing. Standard Western non-disclosure agreements are designed for trade secret disputes between domestic peers governed by common law discovery rules. When applied to Asian contract manufacturing, they protect almost nothing.

To safeguard proprietary technology, custom tooling, and commercial market share, international buyers must abandon boilerplate NDAs and execute an enforceable, bilingual **NNN Agreement (Non-Disclosure, Non-Use, Non-Circumvention)** coupled with a dedicated **Tooling Bailment Schedule**, calibrated to Chinese Civil Code enforcement mechanisms and backed by predetermined liquidated damages.

## 1. Why Western NDAs Fail Completely in Asian Manufacturing

Understanding why a standard Western NDA collapses in overseas sourcing requires examining the divergence between Western legal assumptions and the operational reality of international manufacturing hubs.

### A. The "Non-Disclosure" Semantic Blindspot
In Western legal practice, the word "confidentiality" is understood to encompass protection against competitive theft. However, under strict legal interpretation, a Non-Disclosure Agreement only forbids the recipient from **disclosing** proprietary information to third parties or the public. 

A Chinese factory that receives your CAD drawings under a standard NDA rarely leaks the drawings onto the internet or sells blueprints to a competitor. Instead, the factory does something far more lucrative:
- **Internal Use:** It uses your drawings, tooling geometries, and tolerances to manufacture identical or slightly modified products on its own assembly lines.
- **Direct Circumvention:** It bypasses you entirely, contacting your end-customers or distributors and offering them the identical finished product at a 30% to 50% discount.

Under a narrow Western NDA, the factory has committed no breach of *non-disclosure*. They did not disclose your files to anyone; they simply *used* them.

### B. The Judicial Enforcement Void
Most Western corporate NDAs stipulate governing law in the buyer’s home jurisdiction (e.g., "This Agreement shall be governed by the laws of the State of New York, with exclusive jurisdiction in New York state courts").

To an Asian factory owner with no assets, bank accounts, or real estate in North America or Europe, a New York court judgment is little more than expensive scrap paper. 
- **Lack of Bilateral Treaties:** Mainland China and the United States have no bilateral treaty for the reciprocal recognition and enforcement of civil and commercial court judgments. While China’s Supreme People’s Court issued judicial guidelines in 2022 easing reciprocity requirements for foreign monetary judgments, non-monetary judgments—such as trade secret injunctions, orders for specific performance, and asset seizure orders—remain virtually impossible to enforce against domestic entities.
- **Relitigation Costs:** To enforce an IP claim, the buyer must re-litigate the entire dispute from scratch before a Chinese court. However, because the contract stipulated exclusive New York jurisdiction, the Chinese court may reject jurisdiction altogether, leaving the buyer in a jurisdictional dead zone.

### C. The Trap of Unliquidated "Actual Damages"
Under Anglo-American contract law, liquidated damages clauses are frequently scrutinized or struck down if deemed "punitive." As a result, Western NDAs typically provide that the non-breaching party may recover "actual damages and injunctive relief."

In Chinese civil litigation, proving "actual economic loss" (实际损失) or "the infringer's illicit profits" (违法所得) from trade secret theft is an extraordinarily heavy evidentiary burden. The plaintiff must produce auditable forensic accounting showing exact sales volumes, profit margins, and direct causal links. In fast-moving hardware and commercial manufacturing, gathering this evidence from a privately held offshore entity is nearly impossible, resulting in token damage awards that do not even cover legal fees.

## 2. The Three Pillars of an Enforceable NNN Agreement

A true NNN Agreement replaces the single pillar of Western confidentiality with three interrelated, non-negotiable negative covenants designed specifically for contract manufacturing.

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                         THE THREE PILLARS OF NNN PROTECTION                     |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                                                                 |
|  1. NON-USE (禁止使用)                                                          |
|     - Absolute prohibition against using buyer's CAD, designs, or data for any  |
|       purpose other than producing POs for the buyer.                           |
|     - Forbids "ghost shifts", production overruns, and reverse engineering.     |
|                                                                                 |
|  2. NON-CIRCUMVENTION (禁止反跳 / 禁止绕开)                                     |
|     - Prohibits selling directly to buyer's customers, distributors, or reps.   |
|     - Bans selling duplicate products on Alibaba, 1688, Amazon, or retail.      |
|     - Extends restriction to factory owners, affiliates, and family proxies.    |
|                                                                                 |
|  3. NON-DISCLOSURE (禁止泄露)                                                   |
|     - Traditional confidentiality extending to all employees and contractors.   |
|     - Mandatory flow-down NNN for sub-tier plating, casting, and PCB vendors.   |
|     - Strict data compartmentalization and post-termination file destruction.   |
|                                                                                 |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Pillar 1: Non-Use (禁止使用)
The Non-Use covenant establishes that the factory may use the buyer’s technical information, CAD files, material recipes, and tooling exclusively to fulfill authorized Purchase Orders issued in writing by the buyer. 

Crucially, the covenant must explicitly prohibit:
- **Production Overruns ("Ghost Shifts"):** Running the injection molding machines or CNC line for an extra 12 hours after completing the buyer's batch of 5,000 units to produce 3,000 unbranded or white-label units for domestic wholesale.
- **Reverse Engineering & "Derivative Works":** Making cosmetic alterations (e.g., changing a bezel radius or rib thickness) and claiming the resulting product is an independent, non-infringing design.
- **Component Subcontracting:** Supplying unique subcomponents or subassemblies to other domestic manufacturers who assemble competitive final products.

### Pillar 2: Non-Circumvention (禁止反跳 / 禁止绕开)
Non-circumvention is the commercial shield that protects the buyer’s market. It recognizes that in contract manufacturing, the factory’s competitive advantage is cost, while the buyer’s advantage is customer relationships, branding, and distribution channels.

Under the Non-Circumvention clause:
- The supplier covenants that neither it, nor any affiliated company, parent, subsidiary, shareholder, director, employee, or agent will directly or indirectly sell, quote, or supply products incorporating or derived from the buyer’s design to any existing or prospective customer of the buyer.
- The supplier is barred from listing the products on any B2B or B2C platform, including Alibaba, Made-in-China, 1688.com, Taobao, Amazon, eBay, or Temu.
- The covenant must bind **Related Parties (关联方)**. A common evasion tactic is for the factory director to establish a separate trading company under the name of a spouse, sibling, or cousin to sell the copied goods while keeping the manufacturing entity technically clean.

### Pillar 3: Non-Disclosure (禁止泄露)
While Non-Use and Non-Circumvention restrict the primary supplier, Non-Disclosure restricts dissemination. In modern Asian manufacturing, tier-1 factories rarely perform every process in-house. A CNC machine shop subcontracts anodizing and laser engraving; a plastic molder subcontracts mold-base machining and silk-screening; an electronics assembler subcontracts PCB fabrication and SMT stencil cutting.

The Non-Disclosure covenant must:
- Limit disclosure strictly to employees with a "need to know."
- Require the supplier to execute binding written back-to-back NNN agreements with any sub-tier supplier before transmitting any CAD files, Gerber files, or specifications.
- Require immediate, certifiable destruction of all digital files and physical scrap upon project termination.

## 3. The Power of Agreed Liquidated Damages (约定违约金)

The single most critical operational clause in a cross-border NNN Agreement is the **Liquidated Damages Clause (违约金条款)**. 

### Why Chinese Law Favors Liquidated Damages
While Anglo-American courts are hostile to contract clauses that appear punitive, Chinese contract jurisprudence embraces pre-agreed damages. Under **Article 585 of the Civil Code of the People's Republic of China (《中华人民共和国民法典》第585条)**:
> *"The parties may agree that when one party breaches the contract, it shall pay a certain amount of liquidated damages to the other party according to the circumstances of the breach, and may also agree on the calculation method of the amount of damages resulting from the breach."*

This statutory mechanism provides three decisive tactical advantages:

1. **Elimination of the Loss Calculation Burden:** In court, the buyer does not need to audit the factory’s books or prove lost retail sales in North America. The buyer only needs to prove the fact of breach—for example, presenting a notary-certified purchase of the product from the supplier’s Alibaba storefront or showing unauthorized tooling operation. Once the breach is established, the contractual damages amount is triggered automatically.
2. **Pre-Litigation Property Preservation (诉前财产保全):** Under Articles 103 and 104 of the PRC Civil Procedure Law, a claimant with a liquidated, quantifiable monetary claim can apply for emergency asset freezing *before* or upon filing a lawsuit. Because the NNN agreement specifies an exact figure (e.g., RMB 1,500,000), the buyer’s Chinese litigation counsel can request the court to freeze the supplier’s operational bank accounts and equipment within 48 to 72 hours. Freezing a factory’s payroll and operating accounts creates overwhelming commercial pressure, almost always forcing an immediate, favorable cash settlement.
3. **A Clear Psychological Deterrent:** When a factory director sees a Western NDA mentioning "injunctive relief and damages according to proof in New York," they view the risk as zero. When they see a bilingual contract stating: *"Breach of Section 1 or 2 shall incur liquidated damages of RMB 2,000,000, enforceable in the local Intermediate People's Court,"* they understand that their local bank account can be frozen overnight.

### Calibrating the Liquidated Damages Figure
The damages figure must be set strategically. Under Judicial Interpretations of the Supreme People's Court, if the agreed liquidated damages exceed 30% of the actual loss caused by the breach, the court may reduce the amount upon the defendant's request. 

For mid-sized industrial procurement projects (tooling and production runs valued between USD 50,000 and USD 500,000), a liquidated damages figure between **RMB 1,000,000 and RMB 3,000,000 (approximately USD 140,000 to USD 420,000)** is widely regarded by Chinese courts as commercially reasonable. It reflects the sunk engineering R&D costs, mold commissioning time, and brand damage, while remaining high enough to exceed the factory’s illicit profits from running unauthorized batches.

## 4. The Tooling Bailment Trap: How to Maintain Ownership of Molds and Dies

For buyers of injection-molded plastics, die-cast aluminum, progressive stamping, and cold-forged components, tooling represents the single largest upfront capital outlay. It is also the factory’s ultimate leverage point.

### The Hostage Tooling Playbook
A typical scenario unfolds as follows:
- The buyer pays USD 45,000 for a 4-cavity hardened steel (H13 or S136) injection mold. The quotation states: "Tooling fee: $45,000, paid 50% deposit, 50% upon T1 sample approval."
- After 12 months of smooth production, the factory announces an unprovoked 30% unit price increase, citing raw material and labor inflation.
- The buyer rejects the increase and states they will collect the mold and transfer it to an alternative injection molder in Jiangsu.
- The factory replies with three legal and physical roadblocks:
  1. *"The tooling contract was a service development contract, not a sale of goods. You paid for our engineering expertise; the physical mold base and steel blocks belong to our factory."*
  2. *"The mold is customized to our specific Haitian injection machine platen dimensions and clamping hydraulics; it cannot function elsewhere."*
  3. **The Statutory Retaining Lien (法定留置权):** The factory claims the buyer owes USD 8,000 for mold storage, maintenance, and unpaid raw material inventory. Under **Article 447 of the PRC Civil Code**, a creditor who lawfully possesses a debtor’s movable property may retain the property until the debt is satisfied. The factory asserts a retaining lien and refuses to release the mold.

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                       TOOLING BAILMENT PROTECTION PROTOCOL                      |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                                                                 |
|  1. LEGAL NATURE: BAILMENT (保管 / 寄托)                                        |
|     - Explicit declaration that buyer holds 100% legal and beneficial title.    |
|     - Factory is strictly a temporary bailee (保管人) with no ownership.       |
|                                                                                 |
|  2. STATUTORY WAIVER OF LIEN (放弃留置权)                                       |
|     - Supplier irrevocably waives all rights of retention under Art. 447.       |
|     - Tooling release cannot be withheld due to commercial invoice disputes.    |
|                                                                                 |
|  3. PHYSICAL IDENTIFICATION & PROOF                                             |
|     - Stainless steel asset plate permanently riveted to mold base.             |
|     - Mandatory text: "Property of [Buyer Name], Tool ID: [XXXX]".             |
|     - T1/T2 final milestone payment gated on high-res photo/video proof.        |
|                                                                                 |
|  4. UNCONDITIONAL 72-HOUR RELEASE COVENANT                                      |
|     - Mandatory handover to buyer's logistics agent within 72h of notice.       |
|     - Daily liquidated damages of RMB 10,000 per day for unlawful retention.    |
|                                                                                 |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### The Solution: A Formal Tooling Bailment Agreement
To defeat this tactic, tooling must never be procured through an informal line item on a proforma invoice. It must be governed by a formal **Tooling Bailment Agreement (模具确权与保管协议)** executed prior to wiring the deposit.

The Bailment Agreement must establish four legal and physical facts:
1. **Title vs. Custody:** The buyer is the sole and exclusive legal and beneficial owner of the tooling, including all cores, cavities, inserts, slides, lifters, hot runner manifolds, cooling fittings, and design drawings. The supplier’s status is strictly that of a temporary custodian/bailee (保管人).
2. **Express Waiver of Retaining Lien (放弃留置权):** The agreement must contain an explicit, unequivocal waiver: *"The Supplier irrevocably waives any and all rights to retain, hold, lien, or encumber the Tooling under Article 447 of the Civil Code of the People's Republic of China or any other statutory or common law provision, regardless of any pending dispute regarding commercial invoices, storage, or manufacturing fees."*
3. **Physical Asset Tagging (资产金属铭牌):** The contract must require the factory to permanently rivet an engraved stainless steel identification plate onto the exterior mold base prior to final payment. The plate must state in English and Chinese:
   > *"Property of [Buyer Company Name]. Asset ID: [SR-TL-2026-001]. Unauthorized removal, operation, or disposal is strictly prohibited and subject to civil liability and criminal prosecution under PRC law."*  
   The final tooling milestone payment (typically 20% to 30%) must be strictly contingent upon receiving high-resolution photographic and video proof of the plate riveted to the mold base, alongside a signed Tooling Handover and Acceptance Certificate.
4. **Guaranteed Release Protocol:** The supplier must agree to pack the tooling for export transport (applying VCI anti-corrosion oil and wooden crating complying with ISPM 15) and release it to the buyer’s designated logistics carrier within 72 hours of receiving written notice, without requiring prior judicial intervention. A per-day liquidated damage (e.g., RMB 10,000 per day) must apply to any refusal to release.

## 5. Governing Law, Language, and Jurisdiction Strategy

A well-drafted NNN agreement is useless if filed in the wrong forum or written in the wrong language.

### A. Governing Law: PRC Law
The agreement should be governed by the laws of the People’s Republic of China (PRC Law). When an agreement between a foreign entity and a domestic manufacturer specifies Chinese law, Chinese judges can apply domestic statutory provisions (such as Civil Code Articles 585 and 447) directly and swiftly. If foreign law is selected, the plaintiff must prove foreign law through costly expert witnesses and consular authentications—a procedural quagmire that can delay emergency relief by 6 to 12 months.

### B. Language: Bilingual Dual-Column with Chinese Prevailing
The agreement must be drafted in dual-column English and Simplified Chinese, with a clear provision stating that the **Chinese version shall prevail in the event of any discrepancy (以中文版本为准)**.
- If an agreement is written solely in English, or if English prevails, Chinese courts must appoint an official translation agency to translate the entire document into Chinese before hearing the case.
- Official court translations frequently mistranslate specialized common-law concepts (e.g., confusing "indemnification" with "compensation for tortious damage," or rendering "circumvention" into vague colloquial phrases), blunting the force of your clauses.
- Providing an authoritative Chinese text drafted by bilingual counsel ensures that the judge reads your exact intended legal terminology.

### C. Forum Selection: Court Litigation vs. Arbitration
Buyers must choose between two distinct dispute resolution mechanisms:

| Strategy Aspect | Chinese People's Court Litigation | Commercial Arbitration (CIETAC / SCIA / HKIAC) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Advantage** | Immediate access to **Pre-Litigation Property Preservation** (freezing bank accounts within 48–72h). | Neutral tribunal, confidential proceedings, highly experienced bilingual arbitrators. |
| **Jurisdiction Choice** | Intermediate People's Court at the **Supplier's registered domicile (被告住所地中级人民法院)**. | CIETAC (Beijing/Shanghai), SCIA (Shenzhen), or HKIAC (Hong Kong). |
| **Enforcement Speed** | Local court enforces its own judgments directly through execution officers. | Award must be taken to the local court for recognition and execution. |
| **Cost Profile** | Lower statutory court fees; plaintiff must advance preservation bond/insurance. | Higher filing and tribunal fees; lower risk of local protectionism in tier-3 cities. |
| **Recommended Use** | **Standard manufacturing, precision tooling, and components** where immediate bank asset freeze is paramount. | **High-stakes IP licensing, joint development (JDA), and complex electronics firmware.** |

For standard manufacturing and tooling protection, **litigation before the Intermediate People's Court at the supplier's domicile** is generally the superior deterrent. The ability of the local court to execute immediate asset freezes against the factory's primary operating accounts outweighs the benefits of arbitration.

## 6. The 5-Step Pre-RFQ Execution Playbook

Executing an NNN agreement must follow a strict chronological sequence. Releasing technical files before the contract is fully executed destroys legal protection.

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                       5-STEP PRE-RFQ EXECUTION PLAYBOOK                         |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                                                                 |
|  STEP 1: CORPORATE ENTITY PENETRATION                                           |
|  - Verify official Chinese corporate name (企业名称) and USCI (统一社会信用代码).|
|  - Ensure signing party is the manufacturing entity, not an offshore shell.     |
|                                                                                 |
|  STEP 2: BILINGUAL NNN EXECUTION BEFORE FILE RELEASE                            |
|  - Sign and seal dual-column NNN with company chop (公章) & legal rep signature.|
|  - Only release 2D/3D CAD models after receiving original signed scan.          |
|                                                                                 |
|  STEP 3: PRE-EMPTIVE DEFENSIVE IP REGISTRATION                                  |
|  - Register English/Chinese brand names in China (Class 7, 9, 11, etc.).        |
|  - File design patents or utility models in China before public disclosure.     |
|                                                                                 |
|  STEP 4: TOOLING FABRICATION GATING & NAMEPLATE AUDIT                           |
|  - Execute Tooling Bailment Agreement prior to tooling deposit.                 |
|  - Gate final 20% tooling payment on video audit of riveted steel asset plate.  |
|                                                                                 |
|  STEP 5: PROACTIVE MARKET & CUSTOMS MONITORING                                  |
|  - Record registered trademarks with China Customs (海关知识产权备案).          |
|  - Run monthly image scrapers across Alibaba, 1688, Taobao, and Amazon.         |
|                                                                                 |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Step 1: Corporate Entity Penetration
Never sign an agreement with an English trade name (e.g., "Shenzhen Super Precision Co., Ltd."). English trade names have no legal status in Chinese courts.
- Demand the supplier’s official Business License (营业执照).
- Extract the official Chinese registered corporate name (e.g., 深圳市精密机械制造有限公司) and the 18-digit **Unified Social Credit Identifier (统一社会信用代码, USCI)**.
- Verify on the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System (国家企业信用信息公示系统, gsxt.gov.cn) that the company is actively operating, has not been placed on the abnormal operations list, and that the signatory is either the Legal Representative (法定代表人) or holds an authorized Power of Attorney (法定代表人授权委托书).

### Step 2: Formal Execution: The Company Chop (公章)
In China, a signature alone by a sales manager does not legally bind the corporation. Under Chinese corporate law, the primary symbol of corporate commitment is the official **Company Chop (公章)**—the round red seal registered with the local Public Security Bureau, bearing the company's full legal Chinese name and identification code.
- The NNN Agreement must be signed by the Legal Representative AND stamped with the official corporate seal.
- Ensure the seal is the primary Company Chop (公章) or Contract Chop (合同专用章), NOT an informal "Financial Seal" (财务章) or "Shipping Seal" (报关章).

### Step 3: Defensive IP Registration in China
An NNN agreement is a contract between two private parties; it does not bind third parties. Because China operates on a strict **First-to-File** system for trademarks and patents:
- File your trademark in China (both English brand name and Chinese transliteration) in relevant Madrid Protocol or direct CTMO classes *before* contacting factories. Registration costs under USD 500 per class and eliminates the risk of trademark squatting.
- If your product has unique aesthetic geometry, file a Chinese Design Patent (外观设计专利) prior to public disclosure.

### Step 4: Tooling Milestone Gating
Never release 100% of tooling funds upon T1 plastic sample approval. Maintain a strict four-stage payment schedule:
- 40% Deposit upon Tooling Bailment Agreement execution and CAD freeze.
- 30% upon T1 physical sample inspection and CMM dimensional verification report.
- 10% upon T2 textured sample approval and pilot production run.
- **20% Retention:** Payable only after:
  a) The factory submits high-resolution photos and video showing the engraved stainless steel asset identification plate permanently riveted to the mold base;
  b) Tooling drawings (2D/3D final as-built mold drawings) and mold maintenance logbook are delivered to the buyer;
  c) The supplier signs the Tooling Handover and Acceptance Certificate.

### Step 5: Customs IP Recordal & Breach Monitoring
Once your trademark and design patents are granted in China, record them with the **General Administration of Customs of China (GACC / 海关总署知识产权海关保护系统)**. 
- When your IP is recorded with China Customs, customs officers at export ports (Yantian, Ningbo, Shanghai, Qingdao) have statutory authority to inspect export containers and impound unauthorized shipments bearing your brand or patented design, blocking infringing goods at the Chinese border before they reach foreign markets.

## 7. Bilingual Clause-by-Clause Contract Template

Below are core model clauses for incorporation into an enforceable cross-border NNN and Tooling Bailment Agreement. They provide side-by-side English and Chinese text, structured for immediate deployment.

### Clause 1: Non-Use (禁止使用)
> **English:**  
> The Receiving Party covenants and agrees that it shall not, directly or indirectly, use the Disclosing Party's Confidential Information, technical drawings, CAD/STEP models, product designs, or proprietary specifications for any purpose other than manufacturing the Products specifically ordered by the Disclosing Party pursuant to written Purchase Orders issued hereunder. Without limiting the generality of the foregoing, the Receiving Party shall not: (a) produce any unauthorized overruns, surplus goods, or "ghost shift" products; (b) manufacture, assemble, or distribute any products that copy, incorporate, or derive from the Disclosing Party's designs, whether with cosmetic modifications or otherwise; or (c) supply any components incorporating the Disclosing Party's proprietary geometry to any third party.  
>  
> **中文：**  
> 接收方承诺并同意，其不得直接或间接将披露方的保密信息、技术图纸、CAD/STEP三维模型、产品设计或专有技术规范用于除依据披露方书面采购订单为披露方生产指定产品之外的任何目的。在不限制前述规定的普遍性的前提下，接收方不得：(a) 生产任何未经授权的超量产品、多余货物或“影子班次/幽灵工单”产品；(b) 制造、组装或分销任何复制、包含或源自披露方设计的产品（无论是否进行外观细微修改）；或 (c) 向任何第三方供应包含披露方专有几何构型或特征的零部件。

### Clause 2: Non-Circumvention (禁止反跳 / 禁止绕开)
> **English:**  
> The Receiving Party covenants that neither it, nor any of its Related Parties (including its parent company, subsidiaries, affiliates, shareholders, directors, officers, key employees, or legal representatives, or any entity owned or controlled by family members thereof), shall, directly or indirectly: (a) solicit, contact, quote, sell to, or accept orders from any customer, distributor, reseller, or retail channel of the Disclosing Party; (b) advertise, list, or sell the Products or substantially similar derivative products on any domestic or international e-commerce platform, including but not limited to Alibaba, 1688.com, Made-in-China, Taobao, Amazon, eBay, Temu, or AliExpress; or (c) circumvent the Disclosing Party by establishing direct commercial relationships with any third party introduced by the Disclosing Party.  
>  
> **中文：**  
> 接收方承诺，其自身及其任何关联方（包括其母公司、子公司、关联实体、股东、董事、高级管理人员、核心员工或法定代表人，或由其家庭成员拥有或控制的任何实体），均不得直接或间接：(a) 招揽、接触、报价、销售予或接受来自披露方的任何客户、分销商、转售商或零售渠道的订单；(b) 在任何国内或国际电子商务平台上（包括但不限于阿里巴巴、1688、中国制造网、淘宝、亚马逊、eBay、Temu或速卖通）宣传、刊登或销售本产品或实质上相似的衍生产品；或 (c) 通过与披露方所介绍的任何第三方建立直接商业合作关系而绕开披露方。

### Clause 3: Prohibition on IP Squatting (禁止抢注知识产权)
> **English:**  
> The Receiving Party acknowledges and agrees that all intellectual property rights in and to the Confidential Information, drawings, tooling, designs, trademarks, and trade names belong exclusively to the Disclosing Party. The Receiving Party covenants that neither it nor any of its Related Parties shall, anywhere in the world (including within the People's Republic of China), file or cause to be filed any application for patents (including invention, utility model, and design patents), trademarks, copyrights, domain names, or other intellectual property registrations that incorporate, reflect, or derive from the Disclosing Party's Confidential Information, or that are identical or confusingly similar to the Disclosing Party's marks. Any registration obtained in violation of this Clause shall be immediately transferred to the Disclosing Party at the Receiving Party's sole expense.  
>  
> **中文：**

## 8. Buyer's Field Conclusion: Contracts That Enforce Themselves

In cross-border sourcing, contracts do not protect you because you intend to spend three years litigating in a foreign tribunal. Contracts protect you when they alter the supplier’s immediate cost-benefit calculus at the workshop level.

A standard Western NDA provides zero deterrence because the factory knows you cannot reach them. A bilingual NNN Agreement with Chinese governing law, intermediate court jurisdiction, explicit tooling bailment, and specific liquidated damages changes everything. 

When the factory director realizes that unauthorized production or refusal to return tooling triggers an immediate, notary-certified claim for RMB 1,500,000 backed by a 48-hour freeze on their operational bank accounts, the mold stays on the shelf, the CAD files remain secure, and the product is manufactured exclusively for the buyer who paid for it. Protect your technology before you send the STEP file—not when the duplicate arrives on Amazon.
