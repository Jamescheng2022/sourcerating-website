## Post text

A coating-thickness reading can be accurate — and still tell you surprisingly little about the steel you are buying.

Here is the gap I would look for in a galvanizing inspection file.

The supplier sends an ASTM A123 certificate and a photograph of a thickness gauge. Neither needs to be fake to leave an important question unanswered:

**Which pieces do those readings actually represent?**

Fabricated steel may pass through a fabricator, an outside galvanizer, an inspector and a packing yard. At each handoff, someone has to preserve the connection between the approved drawing, the galvanizing lot, the sampled pieces and the material being shipped.

A representative sampling plan is legitimate. Demanding a reading on every item is not necessarily the right answer. But a sample without a clear lot and piece identity is difficult to use when a buyer needs to release an actual shipment.

A practical request is to ask for four connected records:

• Approved drawing revision and piece-mark register
• Galvanizing lot linked to those pieces
• Representative coating measurements and the acceptance basis
• Packing list showing which released pieces are on the truck

A standard tells you how to assess the coating. It does not automatically do your traceability work for you.

The better question is not “Can the supplier send another certificate?” It is “Can the certificate be traced to my shipment?”
